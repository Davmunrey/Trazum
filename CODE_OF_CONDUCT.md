# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is trying to make the thing better. Argue
about the work.

## What is expected

**Argue about the code, the number, or the decision.** This project's whole
subject is being wrong in public — every release names what it got wrong, and
the changelog records reversals as readily as additions. That only works if
being wrong is safe. Somebody who says "this figure is wrong and here is why"
is doing the most valuable thing anybody does here.

**Say what you mean plainly.** Directness is welcome. Contempt is not, and the
difference is whether the sentence is about the work or about the person.

**Take a maintainer's "no" as an answer**, and expect a reason with it — that
standard is written into the product and it applies to the people too. A
refusal here never arrives bare.

## What is not tolerated

Harassment of any kind. Personal attacks, demeaning comments, or comments about
somebody's identity rather than their argument. Publishing somebody's private
information. Sustained disruption of a discussion.

This applies in issues, pull requests, commit messages, code review and
anywhere else the project's name is attached.

## Reporting

Email **davidmunozrey@protonmail.com**. Reports are read by the maintainer and are
not shared further without the reporter's agreement.

If the report concerns the maintainer, say so — there is one maintainer, and
pretending otherwise would make this section a formality rather than a route.
That is a real limitation of a single-maintainer project and it is better
stated than papered over.

## What happens next

A first response within a week. Depending on what happened: a private word, a
public correction, edited or removed content, or a block from the repository.
Whatever is decided is explained to the person it affects.

## Scope

This applies to everyone taking part, including the maintainer, and to
project spaces and to public spaces where somebody is representing the project.
